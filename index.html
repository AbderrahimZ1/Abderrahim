01 · Most cited flagshipSafety Science · 2023

Predicting fatal fall from heights accidents using random forest classification machine-learning model

A. Zermane, M.Z.M. Tohir, H. Zermane, M.R. Baharudin, H.M. Yusoff

Seven machine-learning models were evaluated using 3,321 accident scenarios. Random Forest was selected to connect site, management, individual and agent factors with fatal-fall predictions.

Research in action

Models are only useful when assumptions, uncertainty and decisions remain visible.

My work spans urban fire spread, incident reconstruction, occupational safety and research software. Across those areas, I prioritise traceable evidence, explicit limitations and outputs that engineers, reviewers and decision-makers can interrogate.

  • Physics-based and reduced-order fire modelling
  • Fault Tree, Event Tree and Bow-Tie reasoning
  • Explainable machine learning and statistical pattern analysis
  • Human-in-the-loop review and auditable evidence systems

01

Research focus

Three connected areas define the work: learning from incidents, modelling fire hazards, and building systems that keep evidence and judgement visible.

A

Incident evidence and prevention

Fatal-accident records are reorganised into patterns, causal pathways and interpretable predictive models rather than treated as isolated statistics.

  • Fault Tree Analysis and causal reconstruction
  • Random forest and comparative classification
  • Prevention priorities grounded in observed cases
B

Fire physics and consequence reasoning

Urban and hydrogen fire research connects sparse evidence to screening-level physical quantities, exposure pathways, tenability and uncertainty.

  • Urban fire-spread scenarios
  • Hydrogen heat-release and flame estimates
  • Explicit assumptions and uncertainty bounds
C

Evidence-led decision systems

Research software preserves source pages, assumptions, confidence and reviewer correction so automation supports professional judgement rather than hiding it.

  • SEED EIA environmental review
  • Design-safety and QHSE assurance
  • Auditable, human-in-the-loop outputs

Flagship publications

The order reflects the established impact of the occupational-safety programme and its progression into dedicated fire-safety research.

01 · Primary flagshipSafety Science · 2023

Predicting fatal fall from heights accidents using random forest classification machine-learning model

A. Zermane, M.Z.M. Tohir, H. Zermane, M.R. Baharudin and H.M. Yusoff

Seven classification methods were tested using 3,321 accident scenarios. Random Forest was selected because it combined strong predictive performance with a practical view of how site, management, individual and agent factors influence fatal-fall outcomes.

3,321 structured accident scenariosSeven competing machine-learning modelsFirst-author contribution in Safety Science

02 · Risk-engineering flagshipSafety Science · 2022

Risk assessment of fatal accidents due to work-at-heights activities using fault tree analysis

A. Zermane, M.Z.M. Tohir, M.R. Baharudin and H.M. Yusoff

The paper reconstructed pathways from 128 fatal cases. It showed that inadequate supervision and leadership, failure to use personal protective equipment, and absent or poorly followed work standards were recurrent system-level prevention priorities.

89.84% — supervision and leadership85.93% — PPE not used85.15% — work standards absent or not followed

03 · First fire-safety publicationFire Technology · 2026

Linking Accident Data and Physics-Based Modeling for Hydrogen Fire Safety

A. Zermane · Fire Technology 62(5), Article 128

This first-author milestone converts sparse hydrogen accident records into screening-level physical descriptors. The framework estimates quantities such as fuel mass, heat-release rate, flame length and explosion-energy potential while propagating uncertainty instead of presenting reconstructed values as exact measurements.

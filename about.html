Research biography

Dr Abderrahim Zermane

Fire and process safety researcher working between hazard physics, incident evidence, explainable analytics and practical decision systems.

Research identity

My work asks how incomplete, complex or administrative safety evidence can be converted into technically defensible outputs. I combine physical modelling, structured risk analysis, statistical methods and explainable machine learning to identify pathways to harm and the controls most likely to interrupt them.

At University College London, I contributed to physics-based urban fire-spread modelling within a wider multi-hazard and fragility framework. The work combined model development with tenability reasoning, validation discipline, uncertainty and scenario communication.

My doctoral programme at Universiti Putra Malaysia focused on fatal falls in Malaysia. Across the associated studies, I moved from pattern analysis to fault-tree reconstruction and machine-learning prediction—three different views of the same prevention problem.

Current direction

The 2026 Fire Technology paper on hydrogen fire safety marks the formal expansion of my first-author programme into fire science. It bridges empirical accident records with screening-level physical reconstruction while keeping missing data and uncertainty explicit.

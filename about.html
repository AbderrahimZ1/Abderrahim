A research programme built around evidence that can be checked

My work began with a practical problem: occupational accident databases contain valuable information, but the records do not automatically explain how failures combine or which controls deserve priority. I therefore used three complementary methods on fatal-fall evidence: pattern analysis to identify who and what was most exposed, fault tree analysis to reconstruct pathways to the fatal event, and machine learning to test whether the recorded factors could support prediction.

That sequence matters. The statistical work described the evidence; the fault trees organised it causally; and the machine-learning studies tested whether those factors could discriminate between accident outcomes. The objective was not to add algorithms for their own sake, but to improve how accident evidence supports prevention.

From occupational risk to fire modelling

At University College London, I worked on physics-based urban fire modelling within a wider multi-hazard and fragility framework. The task extended beyond producing a simulation. It required explicit assumptions, thresholds for tenability and spread, validation discipline, version control and outputs that could be interpreted within comparative city scenarios.

The 2026 paper Linking Accident Data and Physics-Based Modeling for Hydrogen Fire Safety marks a further step in that direction. It uses 954 hydrogen-related incidents to reconstruct screening-level physical descriptors such as fuel mass, heat-release rate, flame length and explosion-energy potential. Because the source records are incomplete, uncertainty is propagated rather than concealed.

Why the software projects belong to the same programme

SEED EIA, Design Safety MY and the QHSE system concepts are not separate from the research philosophy. Each one addresses a recurring weakness in technical review: conclusions become detached from their sources, assumptions are hidden, and automated outputs appear more certain than the evidence allows. The systems therefore preserve page references, confidence, reviewer correction, legal applicability and audit history.

Methods and capabilities

The methods are selected according to the question and the quality of the available evidence.

Accident analysis

Pattern and causal reconstruction

Fatality databases, contributing-factor coding, comparative analysis and Fault Tree Analysis for identifying dominant pathways and control priorities.

Predictive modelling

Explainable machine learning

Model comparison, class imbalance, calibration, feature interpretation and careful separation between predictive performance and causal claims.

Fire safety

Physical and reduced-order modelling

Urban fire spread, hydrogen fire and explosion descriptors, exposure pathways, tenability, uncertainty and scenario comparison.

Spatial analysis

City-scale scenarios

Building footprints, QGIS, local geometry, exposure mapping and integration with wider fragility or multi-hazard workflows.

Research software

Auditable decision support

Evidence chains, page provenance, controlled outputs, reviewer overrides and human-in-the-loop analysis.

Teaching

First-principles explanation

Fire and explosion safety, risk methods, ergonomics and quantitative reasoning translated into practical exercises and design work.

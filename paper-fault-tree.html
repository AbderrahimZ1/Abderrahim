Flagship publication · Safety Science · 2022

Risk assessment of fatal accidents due to work-at-heights activities using fault tree analysis

First author: A. Zermane · with M.Z.M. Tohir, M.R. Baharudin and H.M. Yusoff.

128 fatal casesFault Tree AnalysisSystem-level prevention priorities
Study figure

What the study reconstructed

Accident narratives were coded into contributing factors, immediate causes and root causes, then assembled into fault-tree pathways. This made it possible to examine combinations of failure rather than treating every cause as independent.

Why the method matters: a fatal fall usually emerges from interacting failures. Fault Tree Analysis makes those combinations visible and shows where upstream controls may interrupt the pathway.

Open the DOI →

Evidence and method

The study used 128 Malaysian fatal work-at-height cases. Accident information was coded into contributing factors, immediate causes and root causes, then combined within fault-tree logic and prioritisation matrices.

Main findings

The highest-occurrence priorities included inadequate supervision and leadership at 89.84%, failure to use personal protective equipment at 85.93%, and absent or poorly followed work standards at 85.15%.

How to interpret those percentages

The percentages do not mean that one factor alone causes a fatal fall. The fault-tree structure shows how management, work-process and immediate-event failures can combine to reach the top event. The value lies in the pathway, not only the frequency.

Prevention significance

The framework shifts attention upstream. Instead of focusing only on worker behaviour at the final moment, it identifies supervision, standards, control implementation and work planning as recurring points where the accident pathway may be interrupted.

Boundary of the analysis

The fault trees depend on the information available in accident records and on the coding framework used to organise that information. They are structured representations of recurring pathways, not proof that every case followed one identical sequence.

The figure is presented as an author-provided research visual. The published paper remains the source for the coding framework, logic gates, pathway definitions and full limitations.

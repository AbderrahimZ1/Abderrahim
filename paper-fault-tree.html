Flagship publication · Safety Science · 2022

Risk assessment of fatal accidents due to work-at-heights activities using fault tree analysis

First author: A. Zermane · with M.Z.M. Tohir, M.R. Baharudin and H.M. Yusoff.

128 fatal casesFault Tree AnalysisSystem-level prevention priorities
Paper figure

What the study reconstructed

Accident narratives were coded into contributing factors, immediate causes and root causes, then assembled into fault-tree pathways. This allowed the analysis to examine combinations of failure rather than treating every cause as independent.

Open the DOI →

Main findings

The highest-occurrence priorities included inadequate supervision and leadership at 89.84%, failure to use personal protective equipment at 85.93%, and absent or poorly followed work standards at 85.15%.

Interpretation

Those percentages do not mean that one factor alone causes a fatal fall. The fault-tree structure shows how management, work-process and immediate-event failures can combine to reach the top event.

Practical value

The framework helps shift attention upstream. Instead of focusing only on worker behaviour at the final moment, it identifies supervision, standards and control implementation as recurring points where the accident pathway may be interrupted.

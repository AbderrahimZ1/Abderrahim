Predicting fatal fall from heights accidents using random forest classification machine-learning model

First author: A. Zermane · with M.Z.M. Tohir, H. Zermane, M.R. Baharudin and H.M. Yusoff.

3,321 accident scenariosSeven models comparedRandom Forest selected
Study figure

What the study tested

The study asked whether structured occupational accident records could support prediction of factors associated with fatal falls. Seven classifiers were compared across site, management, individual and agent-related factor groups.

Why the comparison matters: a single model can look impressive without a fair baseline. Comparing seven approaches made the selection defensible rather than arbitrary.

Open the DOI →

Evidence and method

Accident records were transformed into 3,321 structured scenarios containing contextual information, direct and root causes, and contributing factors. The models were evaluated comparatively across factor groups rather than presenting one algorithm without a reference point.

Main finding

Random Forest provided the strongest overall balance for the dataset and enabled factor-level interpretation through feature importance. The model was therefore useful not only for classification, but also for examining which recorded factors shaped the predictions.

What the result supports

The result supports screening, prioritisation and further investigation. It shows that administrative accident datasets can be organised into a repeatable predictive workflow and can reveal combinations of factors that deserve closer preventive attention.

What the result does not prove

Feature importance is not causal proof. The model can inherit missing information, inconsistent categories and reporting bias from the source records. It does not replace a competent incident investigation, engineering judgement or site-specific risk assessment.

Practical value

The model demonstrates how large accident datasets can be used to focus prevention questions where manual review would otherwise be difficult. Its strongest value is helping analysts ask better questions about recurring management, site, worker and agent conditions.

Predicting fatal fall from heights accidents using random forest classification machine-learning model

First author: A. Zermane · with M.Z.M. Tohir, H. Zermane, M.R. Baharudin and H.M. Yusoff.

3,321 accident scenariosSeven models comparedRandom Forest selected
Paper figure

What the study tested

The study asked whether structured occupational accident records could support prediction of factors associated with fatal falls. Seven classifiers were compared across site, management, individual and agent-related factor groups.

Open the DOI →

Method

Accident records were transformed into 3,321 structured scenarios containing contextual information, direct and root causes, and contributing factors. The models were evaluated comparatively rather than presenting a single algorithm without a baseline.

Main finding

Random Forest provided the strongest overall balance for the dataset and enabled factor-level interpretation through feature importance. The result supports screening and prioritisation, not causal proof and not replacement of incident investigation.

Practical value

The model demonstrates how large administrative accident datasets can be used to identify recurring combinations of risk factors and focus prevention questions where manual review would otherwise be difficult.

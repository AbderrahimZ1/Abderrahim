Linking Accident Data and Physics-Based Modeling for Hydrogen Fire Safety

First author: A. Zermane · Fire Technology 62(5), Article 128.

954 hydrogen incidentsPhysics-based reconstructionMonte Carlo uncertainty
Paper figure

What the framework does

Four HIAD 2.1 tables were consolidated into a master incident dataset. First-order engineering relationships were then used to estimate fuel inventory, heat-release rate, flame length, explosion-energy potential and notional blast indicators.

Open the DOI →

Why reconstruction was necessary

Hydrogen incident databases often contain event descriptions and consequences without the physical quantities needed for engineering comparison. The paper therefore reconstructs screening-level descriptors while recording the assumptions used to fill missing fields.

Main finding

The reconstructed incidents span several orders of magnitude in potential energy release, yet simple relationships with fatalities and damage remain weak. Exposure, confinement, ignition timing, mitigation and missing reporting fields materially affect the observed consequences.

What the paper does not claim

The estimated quantities are not measurements and do not validate a detailed consequence model. Their value is comparative: they create a transparent bridge between incident learning and physics-based interpretation and reveal which fields future incident databases should record.

Research significance

This paper is the third flagship publication and the first dedicated fire-safety paper in the first-author programme, extending earlier evidence-led risk work into hydrogen fire and explosion science.
